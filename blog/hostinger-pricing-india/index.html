₹69 a month is a real price. It is also four years paid in one go, before GST, without a domain, and it becomes ₹289 a month in year five. None of that is hidden — it is all on the page — but it is spread across the page in a way that makes the first number the one you remember.

Here is the whole picture in one place: what Hostinger India actually charges, what the renewal costs, what gets added at checkout, and what a website really costs over four years.

How the pricing model works

Three mechanics explain every number you will see.

  1. The discount is tied to term length. The headline price is the 48-month price for shared hosting and the 24-month price for VPS. Shorter terms cost meaningfully more per month.
  2. You pay the whole term upfront. ₹149/month on a 48-month plan is a single payment of ₹7,152 today, not a monthly debit.
  3. Renewal is at the standard rate. Between three and four times the promotional rate on every plan. This is industry-standard, not a Hostinger quirk, but it is the number to plan around.

The four things added after the headline price

1. GST, 18%

Hostinger states plainly in its India footer that prices are listed without GST. Eighteen percent is added at checkout. So a ₹7,152 Premium plan is about ₹8,439 on your card. If you are GST-registered this is recoverable as input credit, which makes it a paperwork item rather than a cost — the same reclaim logic our GST on ads calculator works through. If you are not registered, it is simply 18% more.

2. The domain, from year two

Every plan except Single includes a free domain, and only on terms longer than a year. It is free for twelve months; after that it renews at the normal registry price — roughly ₹1,000–1,200 a year for a .com. On the Single plan there is no free domain at all, so add it to year one.

3. Email, from year two

The included mailboxes are free for the first year. Keeping them afterwards is a separate line. Decide in month ten, not month thirteen.

4. Checkout add-ons

The cart offers extras — priority support, daily backups on plans that only include weekly, an SEO toolkit, domain privacy. Two are usually worth it and the rest usually are not:

  • Daily backups — worth it if you run a store and your plan only has weekly. Otherwise take the plan that already includes them.
  • Domain privacy — cheap, and keeps your personal address out of public WHOIS. Reasonable.
  • An SEO toolkit — skip. Search Console is free and better; see the four reports worth opening.

The honest monthly cost of a ₹149 plan is about ₹218 once GST and the domain renewal are included. Still cheap — just not ₹149.

Is the four-year term worth it?

A straightforward rule:

  • Take 48 months if the website is for an established business that will exist in four years. The discount is large and real, and the 30-day money-back guarantee covers the "I changed my mind immediately" case.
  • Take 12 or 24 months if this is a new idea, a side project, or a client site where you are not sure the relationship lasts. Paying more per month for the option to walk away is a sensible trade, not a mistake.
  • Never take 1 month unless you are genuinely testing for a few weeks. The monthly rate makes the whole value proposition disappear.

Note what the refund policy does and does not cover: there is a 30-day money-back guarantee on hosting. Domains are generally not refundable once registered, because registration is immediate and irreversible. Check the current terms for your specific order before assuming.

What the upfront payment really means

Paying ₹8,439 today for four years of hosting is not the same as paying ₹176 a month, even though the arithmetic says it is. It is money out of working capital now, and for a small business that is a real consideration — particularly in the first year, when the same amount could be a fortnight of ad spend testing whether the business works at all.

So weigh it the way you would any other prepayment. If cash is comfortable and the business is established, the four-year term is simply cheaper and you should take it. If cash is tight, a 12-month term at a higher monthly rate is the better decision even though it costs more in total — because the flexibility is worth more to you right now than the discount is. There is no universally correct answer here, only the one that matches your cash position.

Three ways to handle renewal

Put a reminder in your calendar for month 46 — two months before the term ends — and pick one:

  1. Renew at the standard rate. Simplest. ₹449/month for Premium is still reasonable for a site that is earning.
  2. Negotiate or wait for a promotion. Hosts run offers constantly, especially around Black Friday and New Year. Contacting support before renewal sometimes produces a better rate; it costs one chat to find out.
  3. Move. If the renewal is more than the site is worth, migrate. A WordPress site moves in an afternoon — the full process is in our migration guide, and it works in either direction.

The failure case is none of the above: auto-renew fires, ₹449 a month appears on a card nobody is checking, and three years later you are paying for a site you forgot. Turn auto-renew off and renew deliberately.

Where Hostinger genuinely is cheap, and where it is not

Being fair about this matters more than cheerleading.

Genuinely strong: the four-year promotional pricing is among the lowest in the Indian market; free migrations are unlimited; the Mumbai data centre is a real advantage for Indian audiences; and hPanel exposes PHP settings that some budget hosts lock down.

Be realistic about: the renewal multiple is steep; the headline price requires a large upfront payment; the Single plan's limits are tighter than they look (no free domain, one mailbox, 200,000 inodes); and "unlimited websites" means unlimited entries in a list, not unlimited capacity — which we take apart in the multiple client websites guide.

How to pay the least for what you need

  • Pick the plan for the next 24 months, not for the business you hope to have. Upgrades are instant; refunds on an over-sized four-year plan are not.
  • Take the longest term you are genuinely confident about — and no longer.
  • Skip the SEO add-on. Use Search Console.
  • If you are GST-registered, make sure the invoice carries your GSTIN so the 18% is reclaimable.
  • Choose the Mumbai data centre. Same price, better performance for Indian visitors.
  • Turn auto-renew off and set the month-46 reminder on the day you buy.

A note on comparing hosts

Every host in this market prices the same way — a deep first-term discount and a standard renewal — so comparing headline prices across providers tells you almost nothing. Compare three numbers instead:

  • The renewal rate, since that is what you pay for most of the site's life.
  • The resource allocation — CPU, RAM and inodes — rather than the marketing words above it.
  • Whether there is a data centre near your customers. For an Indian audience this is worth more than a slightly cheaper plan, and no amount of optimisation buys back the latency of a server on another continent.

Add the cost of leaving: a host that makes migration free and painless is worth a little more than one that does not, because it keeps your next decision cheap.

Verdict

Premium on 48 months is the best value for a small business website in India — about ₹245 a month all-in across four years. Unlimited if there is a store. KVM 2 if you are running your own applications. Single only if you are certain it is one site and you remember to buy a domain.

And then forget about hosting. At these numbers it is not the lever that decides whether the website works. Where you put the rest of the budget is, and if the site exists but the enquiries do not, that is the conversation worth having.
