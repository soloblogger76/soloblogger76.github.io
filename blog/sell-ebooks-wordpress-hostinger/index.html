Selling a digital product from your own website is one of the few online businesses where the infrastructure genuinely is cheap. A ₹149-a-month hosting plan, a free plugin and a payment gateway is the entire stack. What people get wrong is not the hosting — it is the delivery, the receipts and the tax treatment, all of which are invisible until the first customer emails to say the download link does not work.

This is the full setup on WordPress and Hostinger, in the order we would build it.

Why your own site rather than a marketplace

Gumroad, Instamojo, Topmate and the rest are faster to start and take a cut of every sale. Running it yourself means:

  • You keep the customer. Email address, purchase history, the ability to sell the second product to the people who bought the first. On a marketplace you are renting that relationship.
  • Lower fees at volume. An Indian payment gateway charges roughly 2% plus GST on the fee. A marketplace layer on top of that is pure margin loss once you are doing real numbers.
  • SEO. Your product page can rank. A marketplace listing ranks for the marketplace.
  • No platform risk. Nobody changes your terms, your payout schedule or your category placement without asking.

The trade is that you own the support burden. For a first product with ten sales a month, a marketplace is genuinely fine. Past that, bring it home.

Hosting: this is the easy part

An ebook business is light. Pages are cacheable, the catalogue is small, and the only heavy moment is the file download itself. The Premium plan at ₹149/month is enough to launch; move to Unlimited (₹249/month) when you want daily backups and more than three sites. The one thing to watch is storage: 20 GB on Premium is plenty for PDFs and thin for video courses.

Choose the Mumbai data centre if your buyers are Indian — checkout pages cannot be cached, so server proximity shows up directly in how the payment step feels. If you later add video, put the files on object storage or a video host rather than growing the hosting plan to hold them.

Payments, the Indian way

Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU and Instamojo all work. Four things to get right:

  • Complete KYC before launch. Gateway activation takes days, not minutes, and needs PAN, bank proof and usually GST details.
  • UPI first. For an Indian consumer product, UPI will be the majority of your payments. Make sure it is enabled and appears first at checkout, not behind a card form.
  • Do not offer COD on a digital product. Obvious, and WooCommerce will happily let you leave it switched on from a previous setup.
  • Test a real ₹1 transaction end to end, including the webhook that marks the order complete. Most "customer paid but got nothing" tickets are a webhook that was never configured.

On tax: digital goods have their own GST treatment and the rate depends on how the product is classified, so get the SAC code and rate confirmed by your CA before you launch rather than after. If you sell to buyers outside India the rules change again. Delivery: where most setups are quietly broken

Never leave the file publicly reachable

If you upload my-book.pdf through the media library, it sits at a guessable URL and anyone can share it. WooCommerce and EDD both provide a protected downloads folder with an .htaccess rule and serve files through a token instead. Use it, and verify by opening the raw path in a private window — if the PDF loads, you are giving it away.

Set limits that are generous but finite

Five downloads, expiring after 30 days, is a reasonable default. Unlimited and never-expiring is how one purchase ends up on a Telegram channel. Honour genuine re-download requests manually; that conversation is also a sales opportunity.

Stamp the PDF

A footer on each page reading "Licensed to buyer@example.com" costs nothing and dramatically reduces casual sharing. People will forward a clean file; far fewer will forward one with their own email on it.

Fix your transactional email before launch

This is the most common failure of all. By default WordPress sends mail through the server, and those messages routinely land in spam or are dropped outright. Install an SMTP plugin and send through a proper provider — Brevo, Zoho, Amazon SES or your Google Workspace account. Then test delivery to Gmail, Outlook and a Yahoo address. A receipt that goes to spam looks exactly like fraud to the customer.

Open the raw file URL in a private window before you launch. If the PDF downloads, you do not have a product, you have a giveaway.

The page that actually sells it

Hosting and plugins are the easy 20%. The sales page is the rest. What works for digital products in India:

  • A specific outcome in the headline. Not "a guide to fitness" — "a 12-week home workout plan for people with 30 minutes and no equipment".
  • Show the inside. Three or four real page spreads. Buyers of a ₹499 PDF want to see what they are getting.
  • Price with a reason. An unexplained price invites negotiation; a price tied to what it replaces does not.
  • A plain refund policy. Counter-intuitively, saying "7-day refund, no questions" increases net revenue for most digital products because it removes the main objection.
  • Trust signals. Who wrote it, why they are credible, and real buyer feedback. Indian buyers are rightly sceptical of a stranger asking for money — see what actually reduces that scepticism.
  • A short checkout. Every extra field costs completions, as covered in form design. Name, email, pay. That is all a PDF needs.

Build the list, not just the sale

The single highest-return decision in a digital product business is capturing emails from people who did not buy. A free chapter or a checklist in exchange for an email address builds an audience you can sell to repeatedly, instead of paying for every sale.

Then automate the follow-up: welcome sequence, a nudge for people who reached checkout and left, and a note when the second product ships. None of that needs a human once it is built — the pattern is the same one in follow-up automation, and if you would rather self-host the automation layer, n8n on a VPS versus n8n Cloud covers that decision.

Pricing a digital product in India

Two mistakes are common and both are expensive. The first is pricing a serious ebook at ₹99 because that feels safe — it signals low value, attracts the least committed buyers, and makes paid traffic impossible because no acquisition channel works at a ₹99 margin. The second is pricing at ₹2,999 with nothing on the page that justifies it.

What works is pricing against the alternative. If your guide saves someone a ₹15,000 consultation or three weekends of trial and error, say so explicitly and price at ₹499–999. Then add a second, higher tier — the ebook plus templates, or plus a recorded walkthrough — because a meaningful share of buyers will take the larger option when it exists, and that single decision often doubles revenue per visitor without any extra traffic.

Measure three numbers, not thirty

Set up GA4 with a purchase event and tag your links with UTMs. Then watch:

  1. Sales page → checkout rate. Low means the page is not convincing.
  2. Checkout → payment rate. Low means friction, fees or a gateway problem.
  3. Revenue per visitor. The only number that lets you decide whether paid traffic is viable.

A realistic launch checklist

  • Hostinger Premium or Unlimited, Mumbai data centre, SSL active.
  • WordPress, a light theme, WooCommerce with one virtual downloadable product.
  • Payment gateway live, UPI enabled, ₹1 test order completed including webhook.
  • Protected downloads verified, download limit and expiry set, PDF stamped.
  • SMTP configured and receipt delivery tested to three providers.
  • Sales page with inside previews, refund policy, and a short checkout.

  • GA4 purchase event firing and verified.

Frequently asked questions

Which Hostinger plan do I need to sell ebooks?

Premium at ₹149 a month is enough to launch — pages are cacheable and the catalogue is tiny. Move to Unlimited once it is earning, mainly for daily backups and the extra storage. Only add video to the plan if you have to; object storage or a video host is better for large files.

How do I stop people sharing my ebook download link?

Three defences. Use the plugin's protected downloads folder so the file is never at a guessable public URL. Set a finite download limit and expiry — five downloads over 30 days is reasonable. And stamp each page with the buyer's email address, which stops most casual sharing.

Why do my WooCommerce order emails go to spam?

Because WordPress sends mail through the web server by default, and mailbox providers distrust it. Install an SMTP plugin and send through a proper provider such as Brevo, Zoho, Amazon SES or your Google Workspace account, then test delivery to Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo before launch.

Do I need to charge GST on ebooks in India?
