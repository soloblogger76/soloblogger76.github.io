How to move a WordPress website from GoDaddy to Hostinger

The move is not hard. What makes it nerve-racking is that the two things most likely to break are email and search rankings. Here is the order that protects both.

Moving a WordPress site between hosts is not technically hard. What makes people nervous — reasonably — is that the two things most likely to break are the two things a business cannot afford to lose: email and search rankings.

This guide covers the full move from GoDaddy to Hostinger, in the order that keeps the site live throughout, with the specific traps that catch Indian businesses. Budget an afternoon. Most of it is waiting.

Before you touch anything

Four checks, all of which take five minutes and any of which can save you a bad evening.

  1. Where is your email? If you use GoDaddy's Microsoft 365 or Workspace email on the same domain, your email is independent of your hosting but depends on the domain's MX records. Note them down now. This is the single most common way a migration goes wrong.
  2. Where are your nameservers? In GoDaddy's domain settings, check whether the domain uses GoDaddy nameservers or something else (Cloudflare is common). This decides how you switch over later.
  3. Lower your DNS TTL. Set the TTL on your A record to 300 seconds, at least 24 hours before the switch. Propagation then takes minutes instead of a day.
  4. Take your own backup. Hostinger's migration is reliable, but the only backup you control is the one you downloaded. Export the database and the wp-content folder to your laptop.

Option A: let Hostinger do it

Hostinger offers free website migration on all plans, with no limit on the number of sites. For WordPress it is largely automatic: you give hPanel your existing WordPress admin credentials or your old host's details, and it pulls the site across. Hostinger's own figures are that a simple automatic WordPress migration finishes in under two hours, while a full cPanel-based migration usually takes over twenty.

The steps:

  1. Buy the plan and complete onboarding, choosing the Mumbai data centre if your audience is Indian.
  2. In hPanel, open Websites → Migrate Website.
  3. Choose WordPress, enter your site URL and WordPress admin username and password.
  4. Submit, and wait for the confirmation email.
  5. Preview the migrated site on Hostinger's temporary URL before changing any DNS.

Note what they cannot migrate: closed platforms such as Wix, Squarespace and Shopify. WordPress, cPanel-based sites and open-source CMSs such as Laravel and PrestaShop are supported.

Option B: do it yourself with a plugin

Worth doing if your site is small, or if you want to be the one who understands what happened.

  1. On the GoDaddy site, install All-in-One WP Migration or Duplicator and export the full site. (The free version of All-in-One has an import size cap; large sites need the paid extension or Duplicator instead.)
  2. In Hostinger's hPanel, install a fresh WordPress on the new plan.
  3. Install the same plugin on the fresh install and import your export file.
  4. Log in with your old credentials — the import replaces the new site's users entirely.
  5. Go to Settings → Permalinks and click Save twice. This rebuilds the rewrite rules and fixes the "every page is a 404" problem before it happens.

Preview on the temporary URL first. Never point DNS at a site you have not opened yourself.

Testing before you switch

On the temporary URL, check the things that break silently:

  • Home page, a blog post, a category page and a contact form — submit the form for real.
  • Images on older posts, not just recent ones.
  • If it is a store: a complete test order, including the payment gateway's callback.
  • WooCommerce or membership plugin licence keys, which are often domain-locked.
  • The admin area under load — bulk-edit a few posts.

Switching the domain over

You have two routes, and the right one depends on your email.

Route 1: change nameservers to Hostinger

Simplest, and the right choice when your email is also moving or you have no custom DNS. In GoDaddy's domain settings, replace the nameservers with Hostinger's. Before you do this, recreate your MX records in Hostinger's DNS zone, or your email stops the moment the nameservers take effect. This is the mistake. Do not skip it.

Route 2: change only the A record

Safer when GoDaddy or Cloudflare is managing DNS for several services. Keep nameservers where they are and point the A record for @ and www at the IP address Hostinger shows in hPanel. Email, subdomains and any verification records are untouched. For most businesses that already have email set up, this is the route we use.

The first hour after the switch

  • Issue SSL. Hostinger provisions a free certificate once DNS resolves. Check that https:// loads without a warning and that http redirects to https.
  • Fix mixed content. If the old site ran on http anywhere, run a search-and-replace over the database for http://yourdomain → https://yourdomain.
  • Check canonical and redirects. Make sure only one version of the site answers — pick www or non-www and 301 the other. Two live versions is a duplicate content problem you do not need.
  • Re-test the contact form. Outgoing mail is configured per host. Forms that worked on GoDaddy frequently stop sending on a new server until SMTP is set up.
  • Confirm WP-Cron. Scheduled posts, backups and WooCommerce jobs all depend on it.

Protecting your search rankings

A like-for-like move should cost you nothing in Google — same URLs, same content, usually faster. The damage comes from small changes made during the move. Three rules:

  1. Do not redesign while migrating. One variable at a time. If rankings drop, you want to know which change caused it.
  2. Keep every URL identical. If any URL must change, 301 it. A migration is not the moment to tidy your permalink structure.
  3. Tell Google. In Search Console, resubmit your sitemap and use URL Inspection on the home page and two or three key pages. The reports worth watching afterwards are Pages and Crawl Stats — a sharp rise in crawl errors in week one means something did not come across.

Also check your robots.txt. A fresh install can arrive with "Discourage search engines" ticked, which adds a noindex and quietly removes you from Google. Our technical SEO checklist covers the rest of the post-move sweep.

Do not cancel GoDaddy yet

Keep the old hosting for at least 30 days. It costs a little and it buys you the ability to compare, recover a file you forgot, or roll back. Cancel only when: the site has been stable for a month, email has been working the whole time, Search Console shows no new coverage errors, and you have a Hostinger backup you have actually tested restoring.

The domain itself is a separate decision. You can leave it registered at GoDaddy indefinitely and simply point it. Transferring consolidates renewals and usually saves money, but a domain transfer takes 5–7 days and requires unlocking plus an auth code — do it as a calm separate task a few weeks after the site move, never on the same day.

Do it yourself, or have it done?

Hostinger's migration team will do it for free, which makes the decision simpler than it looks. Use the free migration when the site is a straightforward WordPress install and you mainly want it to work. Do it yourself when the site has custom code, several subdomains, or an unusual plugin set — not because the team cannot handle it, but because you will be the one debugging it afterwards and the knowledge is worth the afternoon.

Pay someone when the site earns money and downtime has a number attached to it. At that point the question is not the migration fee, it is who is accountable at 11pm if the checkout does not come back.

A realistic timeline

Day 0: buy the plan, lower the TTL, take your own backup. Day 1: run the migration, test on the temporary URL, switch DNS in the evening when traffic is lowest, then work through the first-hour checklist. Day 2: resubmit the sitemap and watch Search Console. Day 30: cancel the old host.

Done in that order, the move is uneventful — which is the only review a migration should get. Frequently asked questions

Will migrating from GoDaddy to Hostinger affect my Google rankings?

A like-for-like move should cost you nothing — same URLs, same content, usually faster. Damage comes from changes made during the move. Keep every URL identical, 301 anything that must change, do not redesign at the same time, and resubmit your sitemap in Search Console afterwards.

Is Hostinger's website migration free?

Yes. Hostinger offers free, unlimited website migrations on all plans. Simple automatic WordPress migrations usually finish in under two hours; full cPanel-based migrations typically take over twenty. Wix, Squarespace and Shopify cannot be migrated because they are closed platforms.

Will my email stop working when I move hosting?

Only if you change nameservers without recreating your MX records first. This is the single most common migration failure. Either recreate the MX records in Hostinger's DNS zone before switching nameservers, or leave the nameservers where they are and change only the A record.

How long should I keep my GoDaddy hosting after moving?

At least 30 days. Cancel only once the site has been stable for a month, email has worked throughout, Search Console shows no new coverage errors, and you have tested restoring a Hostinger backup. The domain is a separate decision and can stay at GoDaddy indefinitely.
