Duplicate content: what actually causes it

Nobody sets out to duplicate content. It arrives through URL parameters, filters and a forgotten staging site.

Media Puppies · 16 September 2026

There is no duplicate content penalty in the way people imagine. What happens is subtler and still costly: your signals split across several URLs, and Google picks one version — often not the one you'd choose.

Where it comes from

  1. Multiple URL versions. http and https, www and non-www, with and without trailing slash. Pick one, redirect the rest.
  2. Filter and sort parameters. Faceted navigation can generate hundreds of near-identical URLs.
  3. Manufacturer product descriptions. Five stores with identical text. Google keeps one.
  4. Location pages templated with a swapped city name. Not just duplicate — a doorway pattern that can hurt the whole site.
  5. A staging site left indexable. An entire second copy of your website competing with you.
  6. Case-sensitive URLs. /seo-services/ and /seo/ are different URLs on most hosting.

Duplicate content rarely gets you punished. It gets you ignored, which costs the same.

The fixes

  • Self-referencing canonical on every page.
  • Canonical filtered URLs back to the main category — except filter combinations with genuine search demand, which deserve their own indexable page.
  • 301 redirect the non-preferred protocol and hostname.
  • Block staging with authentication, not just robots.txt.
  • Write your own product copy. Reviews on the page help too — unique text that updates itself.

How to find it

Search site:yourdomain.com and look for the same page appearing several times. Then check Search Console's indexing report for Duplicate exclusion reasons, which names the URL Google chose instead.
