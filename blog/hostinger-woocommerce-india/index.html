A WooCommerce store is not a blog with a buy button. It behaves differently on a server in ways that only show up once you have real traffic, and the plan that is perfectly adequate for a content site will embarrass you during your first festive-season sale.

This is a practical sizing guide for Indian small stores on Hostinger's hosting plans — which plan matches which stage, what the limits actually mean, and the five configuration mistakes that cost more performance than any upgrade will buy back.

Why WooCommerce is heavier than it looks

A blog post can be cached once and served to ten thousand people. Large parts of a store cannot be:

  • Cart, checkout and My Account pages must bypass cache. Every one of those page loads is a full PHP execution plus several database queries.
  • Logged-in and cart-holding sessions bypass cache too. A visitor who adds something to the basket stops being cheap to serve.
  • The admin is expensive. Order screens, stock updates and reports hit the database hard, and they run at exactly the time your store is busiest.
  • Background jobs never stop. Scheduled actions, abandoned-cart timers, stock syncs and email queues all consume the same allocation your shoppers do.

So the number that matters is not products or page views. It is concurrent uncacheable requests — roughly, how many people are in your checkout flow at the same moment.

Inodes are the limit that catches Indian stores

Inodes count files, not gigabytes. A 400-product store with six images each, five thumbnail sizes generated per image, plus plugin assets, order exports and cached pages, reaches six figures of files quickly — and the files are small, so your "20 GB of 20 GB free" dashboard looks healthy while you are nearly out of inodes.

Two practical defences: disable the WordPress image sizes your theme does not use, and clean out old order exports and backup-plugin archives rather than letting them stack up for three years.

Where your server should sit

Hostinger has a Mumbai data centre. For a store selling to Indian customers, choose it. Time to first byte from Mumbai to a Delhi or Bengaluru shopper is meaningfully better than from Singapore or the Netherlands, and on mobile networks that difference is visible to real people rather than only to testing tools.

Put Cloudflare in front of it anyway. It is free, it absorbs bot traffic that would otherwise eat your CPU allocation, and it caches static assets at the edge. On Core Web Vitals in Indian conditions, those two choices together usually do more than a plan upgrade.

Most "slow store" complaints we audit are an uncached checkout, a 2 MB hero image and forty plugins — not an undersized hosting plan.

The five things that actually slow an Indian WooCommerce store

1. No object cache

WooCommerce makes a lot of repeated database queries, and a persistent object cache removes most of them. Hostinger's managed WordPress plans include LiteSpeed with object caching, so turn it on — it is off by default on plenty of installs we audit. If you need Redis specifically, or want to tune the cache yourself, that is a VPS job.

2. Page builders on product pages

A page builder on a landing page is fine. A page builder rendering 300 product pages is a tax you pay on every uncached request. Use the theme's native product template and keep the builder for campaign pages — the trade-offs are covered in the Elementor hosting guide.

3. Unbounded product filters

AJAX filters with attribute and price combinations generate expensive queries that cannot be cached. They are lovely for the ten customers who use them and brutal for the server. Limit the facets.

4. Plugin sprawl

Indian stores accumulate: a COD plugin, a GST invoice plugin, a shipping plugin, a WhatsApp plugin, two popup plugins, an abandoned-cart plugin and a reviews plugin. Each adds queries and scheduled jobs. Audit them quarterly and delete rather than deactivate.

5. Scheduled actions piling up

WooCommerce's action scheduler table is the most common cause of a store that is fine for six months and then slow. Check it. A table with hundreds of thousands of completed actions needs clearing.

The India-specific setup checklist

  • Payment gateway. Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree and Instamojo all have maintained WooCommerce plugins. Pick one and remove the others — each one you keep installed loads scripts on checkout.
  • Cash on delivery. If you offer COD, understand what it does to your margin before scaling ads to it. Our COD vs prepaid calculator works out the real cost once two-way shipping on returns is counted.
  • GST invoicing. Decide early whether invoices are generated in WooCommerce or in your accounting software. Retrofitting this after 2,000 orders is miserable.
  • Daily backups. Weekly backups are acceptable for a blog and not for a store — a week of lost orders is unrecoverable. This alone justifies Unlimited over Premium.
  • Staging site. Never update WooCommerce or a payment plugin directly on a live store on a Friday. Use the extra site allowance for a staging copy.

When to leave shared hosting

Four signals, any one of which is enough:

  1. You see 503 errors or "resource limit reached" notices more than a couple of times a month.
  2. The admin is slow while the storefront is fine — that is CPU contention, not a caching problem.
  3. You need Redis, a dedicated cron, or a custom PHP configuration.
  4. A sale day matters enough that you would rather over-provision than find out.

At that point either Cloud Startup or a VPS is right. Cloud Startup keeps the managed panel; a VPS gives you control and costs about the same. If you are weighing the VPS route, the KVM plan comparison explains how those tiers are sized.

Two weeks before a festive sale

Most Indian stores do a meaningful share of annual revenue in a few weeks around Diwali, and that is exactly when hosting decisions get tested. A fortnight before:

  1. Upgrade now, not on the day. Plan changes are instant, but discovering you need one during a traffic spike is not a calm experience. If last year you saw resource warnings, move up a tier before the campaign starts.
  2. Load-test honestly. Ask ten people to browse and check out at the same time while you watch resource usage in hPanel. It is crude and it tells you more than a synthetic speed score.
  3. Freeze deployments. No plugin updates, no theme changes, no new page builder experiments once the campaign is live. Every change is a risk with no upside during your busiest fortnight.
  4. Clear the action scheduler and old transients so the database starts the season clean.
  5. Check backup restore works — not that backups exist. Restore one to a staging site and open it.
  6. Warm the cache after the last change so the first real visitors are not the ones generating every cached page.

The pattern we see every year is that stores spend heavily on festive campaigns and then lose a chunk of it to a checkout that fell over for two hours on the best day. The hosting plan is cheap insurance; the fortnight of discipline is free.

Frequently asked questions

Can I run WooCommerce on Hostinger's Premium plan?

You can launch on it — under 50 products and fewer than ten orders a day is manageable. But Premium gives you one CPU core, 2 GB of RAM and weekly backups, and a store that matters needs daily backups. Unlimited is the realistic floor for a store you depend on.

What are inodes and why do they matter for a store?

Inodes count files, not gigabytes. WooCommerce generates several thumbnail sizes per product image, and plugin assets, order exports and cached pages add thousands more. A 400-product store can approach six figures of files while the storage dashboard still looks healthy.

Should I choose Hostinger's Mumbai data centre?

Yes, if your customers are in India. Cart and checkout pages cannot be cached, so every one of those requests travels to your server and back. Mumbai to Delhi is meaningfully quicker than Singapore to Delhi, and on mobile networks real people notice.

When should a WooCommerce store move off shared hosting?

Four signals: repeated 503 or resource-limit notices, a slow admin while the storefront is fine, needing Redis or a custom PHP configuration, or a sale day important enough that you would rather over-provision. Any one of them is enough.
