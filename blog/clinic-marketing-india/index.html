Marketing for clinics and aesthetics in India without breaking ad policy

Most clinic ad accounts get restricted for the same three things. All of them are avoidable, and the compliant version usually performs better anyway.

Media Puppies · 13 July 2026

Clinics have the hardest advertising problem in India: the thing that converts best — dramatic before-and-after imagery and personal claims — is precisely what gets ad accounts restricted.

The good news is that the compliant approach usually books better consultations, because it attracts people who trust you rather than people chasing a miracle.

What actually gets accounts restricted

  1. Before-and-after images. Prohibited in most health and beauty contexts, whatever your competitors are currently getting away with.
  2. Personal attributes. Copy that implies you know something about the reader — "struggling with hair fall?", "embarrassed by your skin?" — is a violation even when it's accurate. This one catches almost everyone.
  3. Outcome guarantees. Promising a specific result from a medical or cosmetic procedure.

If you're already restricted, the recovery playbook is here. Appealing without fixing the cause doesn't work.

What to run instead

The doctor, on camera

The single strongest asset a clinic has. A ninety-second explanation of a procedure — what it does, who it suits, who it doesn't — outperforms any polished production. It builds trust and it's entirely compliant.

Process, not outcome

Show the consultation, the equipment, the room, the aftercare. People are anxious about the experience as much as the result. Answering that anxiety is both effective and allowed.

Price and duration, plainly

The two questions everyone has and few clinics answer. Stating a range filters out people who can't afford it before they take a consultation slot.

Honest limits

"This works well for X. It doesn't do much for Y." Saying what a treatment won't do is the fastest credibility you can buy, and it reduces refund conversations later.

The compliant ad and the trustworthy ad turn out to be the same ad. That's not a coincidence.

Local search matters more than you think

For a clinic with a catchment area, the map pack is often worth more than paid. Google Business Profile, reviews with text in them, and location pages do work that ads have to keep paying for — the playbook is here.

Optimise for booked consults, not leads

A form fill is not a patient. Send booked-appointment data back to the platform and it starts finding people who actually show up. One clinic we work with dropped raw lead volume by about a third and nearly doubled booked consultations by making exactly this change.

What to measure

  • Cost per booked consultation — not cost per lead.
  • Show-up rate. If bookings are high and attendance is low, the problem is your reminder sequence, not your ads.
  • Consult-to-treatment rate, which tells you whether marketing is attracting the right people.
  • Review velocity, because it compounds into local visibility.
