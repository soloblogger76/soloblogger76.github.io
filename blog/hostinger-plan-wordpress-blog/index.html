What a new blog actually needs

Be honest about the first year. A new WordPress blog is one site, a few dozen posts, a theme, maybe fifteen plugins, and traffic that starts at zero. It needs almost nothing. The temptation is to buy for the traffic you hope to have; the correct move is to buy for the traffic you will actually have, because upgrading mid-term is easy and a refund is not.

By that test, Premium is the right plan for most new blogs, and the reasons have nothing to do with speed:

  • Three websites instead of one. Almost everyone eventually wants a staging copy, a second project, or a client favour. On Single you cannot, and the upgrade costs more than buying Premium did.
  • Two mailboxes per site. Single gives you one. You will want hello@ and you@.
  • Double the storage and inodes. Inodes count files, not size. WordPress plus a page builder plus image thumbnails generates tens of thousands of files quickly.

Pick Single only if you are certain this is one site and nothing else. The gap looks like ₹3,840 over four years, but Single has no free domain, so the real saving is closer to ₹2,740 — about ₹57 a month. Pick Unlimited if you already know there will be several sites — but read the dedicated piece on running multiple client websites on one plan first, because the honest answer there is more complicated.

The three places money quietly leaks

1. The free domain is free for one year

Every plan except Single includes a free domain, and only on terms longer than a year. It is free for the first year only — in year two it renews at the normal rate, around ₹1,000–1,200 for a .com and more for some extensions. Nothing dishonest about it; just put it in the budget rather than being surprised. If you take the Single plan, buy the domain separately and add roughly ₹1,100 to year one.

2. The free email is free for one year

Same pattern. The mailboxes are included for the first year, and continuing them is a separate line item afterwards. If email matters to your business, decide in month ten rather than month thirteen.

3. The term is the real decision

The ₹149/month headline exists only on the 48-month term. On 12 months the monthly price is noticeably higher. So the actual question is not "which plan" but "am I confident enough about this blog to pay ₹7,152 today". If the answer is no, take a shorter term, accept the higher monthly rate, and treat the difference as the cost of optionality. That is a perfectly rational trade.

Buy the term you are confident about, not the discount you are tempted by.

What makes a WordPress blog fast (it is not the plan)

People upgrade hosting to fix speed and are disappointed. On a content blog, the plan is rarely the bottleneck. In the audits we run, the order of impact is almost always:

  1. Images. Unresized photos straight from a phone are the single biggest cause of slow Indian WordPress sites. Serve WebP, size them to the container, and lazy-load below the fold.
  2. Theme and page builder. A heavy multipurpose theme loads hundreds of kilobytes of CSS and JavaScript on every page. A lean theme beats a hosting upgrade every time.
  3. Plugin count. Twelve plugins is fine. Forty is a performance problem and a security problem.
  4. Caching and a CDN. Page caching plus Cloudflare in front costs nothing and does more than doubling your CPU allocation.
  5. Then, and only then, the hosting plan.

Two things to do on day one

Whatever plan you pick:

  • Turn off auto-renew and set a calendar reminder for month 46. You then renew deliberately, at a price you have checked, instead of discovering ₹449 a month on a card statement.
  • Set up Search Console before you publish anything. A blog with no Search Console is a blog with no feedback. Our four-report guide covers the only parts worth opening, and the first 90 days of SEO for a brand new site covers what to do with what you find.

Is managed WordPress hosting different?

Hostinger sells "WordPress hosting" as a separate product page, but on the shared tiers it is the same infrastructure with WordPress-specific tooling layered on: a one-click installer, LiteSpeed with object caching, automatic core updates and an AI assistant in the dashboard. You are not choosing a different server by clicking a different page. Choose the plan on the limits in the table above and the WordPress features come with it.

Genuinely managed hosting — the category where someone else owns updates, staging and uptime for several thousand rupees a month — is a different market, and it is not where a new blog should start.

Five minutes of security on day one

Hosting plans do not protect you from the most common ways a small WordPress site gets compromised, and all of these take minutes:

  • Do not use "admin" as a username. Most brute-force attempts assume it.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for the WordPress login and for the hosting account itself.
  • Delete deactivated plugins and themes. A deactivated plugin still has files on the server, and files can still be exploited.
  • Keep the free SSL active and force https sitewide.
  • Know where your backups are and restore one once, deliberately, while nothing is wrong. A backup you have never tested is a hope, not a plan.

None of that costs money, and it prevents the kind of incident that turns a ₹149 plan into a weekend of work.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Hostinger Single plan enough for a WordPress blog?

Technically yes — one website, 10 GB and 200,000 inodes will run a modest blog. Practically, it has no free domain, one mailbox and no room for a staging copy or second project. Premium costs about ₹57 a month more once you account for the domain and removes all three limits.

What does Hostinger cost after the first term?

Renewal is three to four times the promotional rate: ₹289 a month for Single, ₹449 for Premium, ₹649 for Unlimited and ₹1,599 for Cloud Startup. Turn auto-renew off and set a reminder for month 46 so you renew deliberately.

Do I need Cloud Startup for a blog?

Almost certainly not. Cloud Startup is sized for sites with real traffic or several demanding stores. A content blog on Premium will not approach its CPU or RAM allocation for a long time, and you can move up in a few clicks on the day it does.

Will a better hosting plan make my WordPress blog faster?

Rarely. On a content site the bottleneck is almost always oversized images, a heavy theme, plugin sprawl or missing caching — in that order. Fix those four and a ₹149 plan will outperform a ₹599 plan that has none of them.
