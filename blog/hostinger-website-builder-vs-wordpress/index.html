The useful thing about this comparison on Hostinger specifically is that money is not part of it. The website builder runs on the same Premium, Unlimited and Cloud Startup plans as WordPress, at the same ₹149, ₹249 and ₹599 a month. You choose inside hPanel after you have paid.

That removes the usual distraction and leaves the real question: who is going to look after this website for the next three years, and what will it need to do by then?

What you are actually choosing

The builder is a drag-and-drop editor with 400+ templates and an AI site generator. You describe the business, it produces a site, you edit it visually. Nothing to update, nothing to break, no plugins, no database to worry about.

WordPress is software you install and own. It powers a large share of the web, has a plugin for almost everything, and gives you full control of the markup, the data and the hosting. It also needs maintenance: core updates, plugin updates, backups, and the occasional afternoon when an update breaks a layout.

Where the builder genuinely wins

Nobody technical is involved

This is the honest case for it. If the person updating the site is the business owner between customer calls, a visual editor that cannot break is worth more than any amount of flexibility they will never use. We have seen plenty of WordPress sites left unpatched for two years because no one owned them — that is a worse outcome than a slightly less capable builder site.

You need it live this week

Running ads to a page that does not exist yet is the usual trigger. The builder gets a credible five-page site up in an afternoon. If the alternative is another month without a site, take it.

It is a brochure, not a machine

Five to fifteen pages, a contact form, a gallery, a map. If that is the whole specification and will remain so, WordPress is extra moving parts for no extra outcome.

You want zero maintenance

No updates, no plugin conflicts, no vulnerability notices. For a one-person business this is a genuine saving, not a compromise.

Where WordPress genuinely wins

Content is your acquisition strategy

If the plan is to rank for things people search — and for most Indian service businesses it should be — you will eventually want categories, tags, author pages, structured internal linking, schema markup and a proper editorial workflow. That is WordPress's home ground. The approach we describe in internal linking and schema markup is far easier to execute there.

You need to connect things

CRM, marketing automation, a membership area, a booking system, regional language versions, a custom quote calculator. Builders cover the common cases; WordPress covers yours.

You want to be able to leave

This is the argument people underrate. A WordPress site moves to any host in an afternoon — the whole process is in our migration guide. Getting out of a proprietary builder means rebuilding. You are choosing a host and a platform in the same click, and only one of those decisions is easy to reverse.

You will hire help

Every second freelancer and agency in India works in WordPress. The pool of people who can take over a proprietary builder site is much smaller, and that shows up in both price and availability on the day you need someone quickly.

Can a builder site rank on Google?

Yes — and the people who insist otherwise are usually selling WordPress development. Google ranks pages, not platforms. Hostinger's builder outputs clean pages, generates a sitemap automatically, and lets you set titles, descriptions and alt text. For a local business competing for "interior designer in Noida", that is sufficient, and the things that will actually decide the result are your Google Business Profile, reviews, and whether the page answers the question.

The ceiling appears later. Fine-grained control over canonical tags, redirect rules, custom schema types, hreflang for regional language pages, and the ability to publish several hundred pages with a consistent template — that is where WordPress pulls away. If your SEO plan is five pages and a Business Profile, the builder is fine. If it is a programme of location pages or a content library, it is not.

Pick the builder if the website is a brochure. Pick WordPress if the website is going to be an asset.

The maintenance cost nobody budgets

A WordPress site needs roughly an hour a month to stay healthy: core and plugin updates applied on a staging copy first, a backup verified, a security scan, and a quick look at Search Console. Skipped for a year, that hour becomes a day — updates that have drifted too far apart break things, and an unpatched plugin is the most common way a small Indian business site ends up serving spam links it knows nothing about.

The builder's maintenance cost is zero, and that is not a small feature. Be honest about whether that hour exists in your month. If it does not, and nobody is being paid for it, the platform that needs it is the wrong platform regardless of how capable it is on paper. The worst outcome in this comparison is not choosing the builder — it is choosing WordPress and then abandoning it.

Starting on the builder and moving later

A reasonable plan, as long as you go in knowing the move is a rebuild rather than an export. Keep three things portable from day one and it stays cheap:

  • Your domain — always registered in your own account, never a developer's.
  • Your content — every page's text written in a document you keep, so a rebuild is layout work, not writing work.
  • Your leads — flowing into a CRM or spreadsheet you own, not only into the builder's form inbox. Our note on whether you need a CRM covers the minimum viable version.

Do that and switching later costs a week of design, not a year of lost content.

What about the AI site builder?

Hostinger's builder includes an AI generator: describe the business and it produces a site. It is genuinely useful for getting past the blank page, and the plans include a small number of AI credits — five on Premium, fifteen on Unlimited and Cloud Startup.

Treat what it produces as a first draft of the layout, not of the copy. AI-generated business copy reads like AI-generated business copy, and Indian buyers are quick to notice a page that says nothing specific. Keep the structure, rewrite the words with real detail — your actual prices, your actual service areas, the objection your customers always raise. That one pass is what separates a site that converts from a site that merely exists, and it is the same argument we make in what AI copywriting still cannot do.

Five questions that settle it

If you are still undecided, answer these honestly and the choice makes itself:

  1. Will you publish content regularly? Yes, meaningfully — WordPress. A few pages that rarely change — builder.
  2. Who edits it in month six? You, between customer calls — builder. A marketing person or an agency — WordPress.
  3. Will anything need to connect to it? CRM, booking, memberships, custom calculators — WordPress.
  4. How many pages in two years? Under twenty — builder. Fifty or more — WordPress.
  5. Would rebuilding the site cost you sleep? If yes, choose the platform you can leave with.

What we would tell a client

If you are a small business with no technical person, no immediate content plan and a need to be online quickly — use the builder, and stop treating that as a lesser choice. A live, maintained, honest five-page site beats a half-finished WordPress project every time.

If the website is meant to generate demand rather than confirm you exist — content, landing pages, campaigns, integrations — start on WordPress. You will get there anyway, and arriving twice costs more.

Either way the hosting plan is the same, so the decision is reversible in the sense that matters: you are not locked into a different bill.
